Forward substitution: an example

To provide an example of forward substitution for solving a lower triangular system like

L⋅y=bL \cdot y = bL⋅y=b
(1)

, let’s apply the forward substitution method to a specific 4x4 system.

We start with the lower triangular matrix LLL, the vector bbb, and the unknown vector yyy. In this case,

L⋅y=bL \cdot y = bL⋅y=b
(2)

becomes:

(α11000α21α2200α31α32α330α41α42α43α44)⋅(y1y2y3y4)=(b1b2b3b4)\begin{pmatrix} \alpha_{11} & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ \alpha_{21} & \alpha_{22} & 0 & 0 \\ \alpha_{31} & \alpha_{32} & \alpha_{33} & 0 \\ \alpha_{41} & \alpha_{42} & \alpha_{43} & \alpha_{44} \end{pmatrix} \cdot \begin{pmatrix} y_1 \\ y_2 \\ y_3 \\ y_4 \end{pmatrix}= \begin{pmatrix} b_1 \\ b_2 \\ b_3 \\ b_4 \end{pmatrix}⎝⎛​α11​α21​α31​α41​​0α22​α32​α42​​00α33​α43​​000α44​​⎠⎞​⋅⎝⎛​y1​y2​y3​y4​​⎠⎞​=⎝⎛​b1​b2​b3​b4​​⎠⎞​
(3)

We will now apply the forward substitution steps to each row (i.e., each equation):

1. First Equation (i=1)(i = 1)(i=1):¶

The first equation is simple because only y1y_1y1​ appears:

α11y1=b1\alpha_{11} y_1 = b_1α11​y1​=b1​
(4)
y1=b1α11y_1 = \frac{b_1}{\alpha_{11}}y1​=α11​b1​​
(5)

2. Second Equation (i=2)(i = 2)(i=2):¶

The second equation involves y1y_1y1​ and y2y_2y2​:

α21y1+α22y2=b2\alpha_{21} y_1 + \alpha_{22} y_2 = b_2α21​y1​+α22​y2​=b2​
(6)
y2=b2−α21y1α22y_2 = \frac{b_2 - \alpha_{21} y_1}{\alpha_{22} }y2​=α22​b2​−α21​y1​​
(7)

And we can substitute y1y_1y1​ from the first equation.

Third Equation (i=3)(i = 3)(i=3):¶

The third equation involves y1y_1y1​, y2y_2y2​, and y3y_3y3​:

α31y1+α32y2+α33y3=b3\alpha_{31} y_1 + \alpha_{32} y_2 + \alpha_{33} y_3 = b_3α31​y1​+α32​y2​+α33​y3​=b3​
(8)
y3=b3−α31y1−α32y2α33y_3 = \frac{b_3 - \alpha_{31} y_1 - \alpha_{32} y_2}{\alpha_{33}}y3​=α33​b3​−α31​y1​−α32​y2​​
(9)

And we can substitute y1y_1y1​ and y2y_2y2​ from previous equations.

4. Fourth Equation (i=4)(i = 4)(i=4):¶

The fourth equation involves y1y_1y1​, y2y_2y2​, y3y_3y3​, and y4y_4y4​:

α41y1+α42y2+α43y3+α44y4=b4\alpha_{41} y_1 + \alpha_{42} y_2 + \alpha_{43} y_3 + \alpha_{44} y_4 = b_4α41​y1​+α42​y2​+α43​y3​+α44​y4​=b4​
(10)
y4=b4−α41y1−α42y2−α43y3α44y_4 = \frac{b_4 - \alpha_{41} y_1 - \alpha_{42} y_2 - \alpha_{43} y_3}{\alpha_{44}}y4​=α44​b4​−α41​y1​−α42​y2​−α43​y3​​
(11)

And we can substitute y1y_1y1​, y2y_2y2​, and y3y_3y3​ from the previous equations.

Summary of the Solution:¶

We now have the solution for the vector yyy:

y1=b1α11y_1 = \frac{b_1}{\alpha_{11}}y1​=α11​b1​​
(12)
y2=b2−α21y1α22y_2 = \frac{b_2 - \alpha_{21} y_1}{\alpha_{22}}y2​=α22​b2​−α21​y1​​
(13)
y3=b3−α31y1−α32y2α33y_3 = \frac{b_3 - \alpha_{31} y_1 - \alpha_{32} y_2}{\alpha_{33}}y3​=α33​b3​−α31​y1​−α32​y2​​
(14)
y4=b4−α41y1−α42y2−α43y3α44y_4 = \frac{b_4 - \alpha_{41} y_1 - \alpha_{42} y_2 - \alpha_{43} y_3}{\alpha_{44}}y4​=α44​b4​−α41​y1​−α42​y2​−α43​y3​​
(15)

that can be generalised as:

yi=1αii(bi−∑j=1i−1αijyj)i=2,3,…,Ny_i = \frac{1}{\alpha_{ii}} \left( b_i - \sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \alpha_{ij} y_j \right) \quad i = 2, 3, \dots, Nyi​=αii​1​(bi​−j=1∑i−1​αij​yj​)i=2,3,…,N
(16)
